Every deal is built so incentives point one way

The question every credit committee asks first: once a loan leaves the originator's balance sheet, what stops them from stopping collections? Here's a representative ₦1B pool structure and where each party's return actually comes from.

Senior Notes ₦750M
75% of pool 16% coupon Paid first
Equity / First-loss ₦250M
25% of pool Retained by originator Absorbs losses first

The originator keeps the first-loss piece and stays on as servicer — so their own capital, and their fee income, depend on the same borrowers actually repaying. The senior investor gets paid first; the originator only profits if collections hold up.

For originators & lenders

Turn your loan book into fresh lending capital — without giving up equity.

If you're turning away good borrowers because your capital is tied up in loans still on your books, securitization is the way out — and you don't need to build the legal and compliance function in-house to get there.

  • 01Keep collecting. You stay on as servicer, earn a fee, and keep your borrower relationships intact.
  • 02Keep the upside. You retain the equity tranche — the highest-returning piece of the structure, funded by a fraction of the capital.
  • 03No dilution. This is debt capital against your existing performing loans, not an equity raise.
  • 04We handle the paperwork. SEC filings, SPV formation, trustee coordination, investor reporting.
  • 05Start small. Your first facility is sized to prove the model — not to securitize your whole book on day one.
  • 06A track record for your next raise. A completed securitization makes your next equity or debt round easier to close.

How a transaction actually gets built

This is a sequence — each step depends on what was validated in the one before it.

01

Discovery

Share an anonymized loan tape. We assess it against what institutional investors actually look for — no commitment, no cost.

02

Structuring

We model the pool: tranche sizes, servicing terms, waterfall priority, and eligibility criteria for the underlying loans.

03

Investor validation

We test the structure with DFI and credit-fund partners before either side commits to a live SPV.

04

Formation & close

The SPV is incorporated, SEC filings go in, a licensed trustee is appointed, and notes are issued to investors.

05

Administration

We run the waterfall, monitor servicer performance, and report to investors for the life of the transaction.

Why this, why now

Nigeria's SEC opened a clearer path for private companies to issue debt securities in April 2025, alongside stricter standards for SPVs and fund structures. The mechanics work. What's missing isn't a rule change — it's someone who does the structuring and administration work well, for deal sizes too small for the big houses and too complex for originators to handle alone.

There is no dedicated securitization statute in Nigeria yet, which means every deal has to be built carefully rather than off a template. That's exactly the work we do.

"The gap isn't legal permission. It's execution — someone who shows up, does the structuring properly, and stays on to run it."
