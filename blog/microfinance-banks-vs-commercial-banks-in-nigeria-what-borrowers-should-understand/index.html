Microfinance Banks vs Commercial Banks in Nigeria: What Borrowers Should Understand

The Short Answer

Microfinance banks (MFBs) in Nigeria exist specifically to serve individuals and micro/small businesses that commercial banks often find too costly to underwrite. Commercial banks operate under a different licence and are generally structured around larger customers and a wider range of services. For borrowers, the difference shows up less in the basic idea of "a bank lends money" and more in who each type of bank is built to serve, and how their operations are shaped around that focus.

How Each Type of Bank Is Set Up

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) supervises both microfinance banks and commercial banks, but under different frameworks. Microfinance banks are licensed under a tiered structure that places them in one of three categories — Unit, State, or National — based on the scope of the areas they are allowed to operate in. That tiered structure is the clearest signal that MFBs are a distinct category of institution, not just smaller versions of commercial banks.

The CBN's microfinance policy framework is explicit about the customer segment MFBs are meant to serve: individuals and micro/small businesses that commercial banks often find too costly to underwrite. In other words, the MFB category was created to fill a gap, not to compete head-on with commercial banks for the same customers.

Commercial banks, by contrast, are licensed to operate across a broader customer base, including larger corporates, government entities, and higher-value individual customers. Their structure, products, and internal processes are generally designed for those larger transactions.

What This Means for You as a Borrower

Because MFBs are built around smaller customers and smaller transactions, the application process, documentation expectations, and the kinds of conversations you have with the bank tend to be tailored to that segment. For an individual borrower or a small shop owner, that alignment between the bank's design and your profile can make a meaningful practical difference — not because one bank is "better" than the other, but because they are designed for different jobs.

A commercial bank's underwriting approach, on the other hand, typically reflects the larger customers it is set up to serve. If your borrowing need is on the larger end — for an established business with more substantial turnover, for example — a commercial bank's structure may simply be a closer fit.

Neither of those observations is a recommendation to choose one over the other. It is a description of how the two categories of bank are organised, so you can match your situation to the institution built for it.

Factors Worth Comparing

When you are weighing an MFB against a commercial bank, the categories below are what the structural difference between the two actually shows up in. Treat each as a question to ask the specific institution you are talking to, because terms vary widely.

  • Customer segment fit. MFBs are licensed to focus on individuals and micro/small businesses. Commercial banks serve a wider customer base. The closer your profile matches the segment a bank is built for, the smoother the process tends to be.
  • Licence tier and scope. Under the CBN's tiered structure, MFBs are classified as Unit, State, or National. That classification affects the geographic scope of where the MFB can lend, which can matter if your business or needs cross state lines.
  • Product range. Different licence categories permit different ranges of services. A commercial bank's licence permits a broader product set than an MFB's. Confirm with the specific bank what products it actually offers, rather than assuming.
  • Documentation and turnaround. The amount of documentation and the time to a decision tend to reflect the size and complexity of the loan. Ask each institution directly what its process looks like for a loan of your size, rather than relying on general impressions.
  • Cost and pricing. Pricing varies by lender and by loan type. The only reliable way to compare is to ask each institution for its pricing on the specific facility you are considering.

What the Reference Framework Does Not Tell You

It is worth being clear about the limits of what can be said from the regulatory framework alone. The CBN's microfinance policy framework tells us who MFBs are licensed to serve and how they are tiered. It does not, on its own, give a borrower the specific pricing, turnaround times, or product terms at any particular MFB or commercial bank. Those details vary from institution to institution and change over time.

For anything beyond the structural picture — actual interest rates, fees, processing times, tenors, or product features — the right source is the specific bank you are considering, ideally in writing.

How to Approach the Decision

A useful starting point is to identify which segment you fall into as a borrower. If you are an individual or a micro/small business, an MFB is, by design, the category of institution built around you. If your needs are larger or more complex, a commercial bank's structure is likely to be the closer fit.

From there, the work is the same as it would be with any lender: compare what specific institutions offer, ask direct questions about pricing, documentation, and timelines, and read the loan documentation carefully before committing. The category of bank narrows the field; the specifics still come from the institution itself.

FAQ

Who regulates microfinance banks in Nigeria?

Microfinance banks in Nigeria are licensed and supervised by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) under a tiered structure that classifies them as Unit, State, or National MFBs based on their scope of operations.

Why would a borrower choose a microfinance bank over a commercial bank?

Borrowers who are individuals or operate micro and small businesses are the primary customers microfinance banks are set up to serve — groups that commercial banks often find too costly to underwrite.

Can a microfinance bank handle the same products as a commercial bank?

No. Microfinance banks are licensed under a different framework with a narrower mandate focused on serving individuals and micro/small businesses, while commercial banks operate under a broader banking licence.

Are deposits in Nigerian banks safe in the same way at both types of institution?

Both types of bank operate within the Nigerian regulatory framework, but the specific protections and requirements attached to each licence category differ. Borrowers and depositors should review what applies to the specific institution they are dealing with.

How should a borrower decide between the two?

The right choice depends on the size and type of borrowing you need, the documentation and time you can put into an application, and whether your profile fits the customer segment each type of bank is built to serve.
