Debt consolidation: what it means to combine several loans into one

Debt consolidation is a financial strategy where a borrower takes out a single new loan to pay off several existing debts. By replacing multiple payments with one, it simplifies budgeting and can reduce overall interest costs. However, the suitability depends on individual circumstances and the terms of the new loan.

What is debt consolidation?

Debt consolidation refers to the process of combining multiple outstanding loans or credit lines into one new loan. The borrower uses the proceeds of the new loan to pay off each of the original debts, leaving only the new loan to be repaid. This can include credit cards, personal loans, auto loans, or other forms of borrowing.

How does it work?

The mechanics are straightforward. A lender provides a single loan that is large enough to cover the total balance of the existing debts. Once the new loan is disbursed, the borrower uses those funds to clear each old account. From that point forward, only one monthly payment is required. The interest rate and repayment term of the consolidation loan may differ from the original obligations.

Benefits of consolidating debt

  • Simplified budgeting – One payment replaces several due dates, reducing the risk of missed deadlines.
  • Potential lower interest rate – If the new loan carries a lower rate than the average of the original debts, total interest can decrease.
  • Extended repayment period – Spreading the balance over a longer term can lower each monthly installment, improving cash flow.
  • Reduced stress – Managing a single debt can ease the mental burden of tracking multiple accounts.

Potential drawbacks

  • Higher total interest – A longer repayment period can increase the overall interest paid, even if the rate is lower.
  • Fees and costs – Origination fees, prepayment penalties, or balance‑transfer charges may offset savings.
  • Risk of re‑accumulating debt – After clearing existing balances, new spending can lead to fresh debt.
  • Eligibility requirements – Lenders may impose credit score or income thresholds that not all borrowers meet.

Who might benefit?

Individuals who struggle with multiple high‑interest obligations, experience cash‑flow mismatches, or find it difficult to keep track of several payments may find consolidation helpful. It is also considered by those seeking a single, predictable repayment schedule. However, it is not a one‑size‑fits‑all solution; the decision should be based on a clear comparison of rates, fees, and personal financial goals.

Key considerations before consolidating

  1. Interest rate comparison – Ensure the new rate is genuinely lower than the blended rate of existing debts.
  2. Fee structure – Examine any upfront or ongoing charges that could diminish the benefit.
  3. Repayment term – A longer term reduces monthly payments but can increase total interest.
  4. Impact on credit score – Closing multiple accounts may affect credit utilization and history.
  5. Lender reputation – Choose a regulated institution with transparent terms.

Common types of consolidation products

  • Personal loan consolidation – A standard unsecured loan used to pay off other debts.
  • Balance‑transfer credit card – Transferring high‑interest balances to a card with a promotional rate.
  • Home‑equity loan or line of credit – Using property equity as collateral, often providing lower rates.
  • Debt management plan – A structured arrangement with a credit counseling agency that negotiates terms with creditors.

In Nigeria, microfinance banks (MFBs) are licensed and supervised by the Central Bank of Nigeria under a tiered structure (Unit, State, National) and primarily serve individuals and micro/small businesses that commercial banks often find too costly to underwrite. These institutions may offer consolidation products tailored to their clients, although the terms can vary widely.

How to evaluate a consolidation offer

  • Compare the annual percentage rate (APR) with the effective rates of current debts.
  • Calculate the total cost over the life of the new loan, including fees.
  • Assess whether the monthly payment fits comfortably within your budget.
  • Review any penalties for early repayment or late payment.
  • Verify that the lender is authorized and that the loan agreement is clear.

Frequently asked questions

What is debt consolidation?
It is the process of taking out a single loan to pay off multiple existing debts, resulting in one remaining obligation.

How does debt consolidation work?
You borrow an amount sufficient to cover all outstanding balances, then use those funds to clear each debt. Afterward, you make a single monthly payment to the new lender.

What are the main benefits?
The primary advantages are a simplified payment schedule, potential interest savings, and reduced administrative burden.

What risks should I be aware of?
Risks include higher overall interest if the term is extended, additional fees, and the possibility of accumulating new debt if spending habits do not change.

Who should consider debt consolidation?
It may be suitable for borrowers with multiple high‑interest loans who can secure a lower rate and who are committed to avoiding further debt accumulation.
