Buy Now, Pay Later in Nigeria: How Instalment Credit at Checkout Works

Buy now, pay later in Nigeria is a form of instalment credit that allows a shopper to split a purchase into scheduled payments instead of paying the full amount upfront. At checkout, a customer may select an instalment option, a credit provider may pay the merchant, and the customer later repays the provider in instalments. The specifics depend on the provider, the amount, the repayment term and the assessment process.

How the checkout instalment process works

Although the user experience can vary, the basic flow often involves a few steps. A merchant offers instalment credit at online or in-store checkout. The customer chooses the plan and confirms the purchase. The credit provider pays the merchant, sometimes minus fees or within its own process. The customer then makes scheduled repayments to the provider.

The shopper may see instalment amounts calculated from the total minus any approved credit limit or discount. The number of instalments and due dates are set by the provider. In some cases, repayment may be through a debit card, bank transfer, USSD or other payment method. The exact mechanics can differ.

Approval may depend on the lender's criteria, and not every shopper will be eligible. Merchants may also set limits or require approval before the option is offered.

For the merchant, the presence of instalment credit can affect pricing, margins and fulfilment. For the shopper, it can make higher-value purchases more manageable in the short term, but it also introduces new repayment obligations.

Credit providers and regulation in Nigeria

Instalment credit in Nigeria can involve microfinance banks and other credit providers. Microfinance banks are licensed and supervised by the Central Bank of Nigeria under a tiered structure: Unit, State and National. They primarily serve individuals and micro/small businesses that commercial banks often find too costly to underwrite.

The tiered structure means different levels of supervision and oversight may apply depending on the licence held by the institution.

Digital money lenders are also part of the consumer credit landscape. The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission, together with the Central Bank of Nigeria, issued guidelines in 2022. The guidelines require digital money lenders in Nigeria to register and be approved before operating. The FCCPC issued the guidelines following widespread complaints about predatory loan apps.

These regulatory elements matter because BNPL depends on credit providers that must assess risk, extend credit and manage repayment. The CBN oversight of microfinance banks and the FCCPC guidelines for digital money lenders are part of the wider framework around consumer credit in Nigeria.

What determines instalment credit at checkout

Several factors shape how instalment credit works for a given transaction. The amount of the purchase, the repayment term, the instalment amount and the repayment frequency all matter. The lender's credit assessment criteria also matter, including credit history and affordability. Costs and terms vary by lender and loan type, so consumers and merchants should review the agreement.

For shoppers, the decision to use BNPL may depend on whether the total cost is manageable and whether the repayment schedule fits their cash flow. For merchants, offering instalments can increase conversion, but it also affects risk and accounting.

How BNPL differs from traditional loans

BNPL is often marketed as a short-form credit product. It may be unsecured or secured depending on the provider, though the structure can vary. It may be offered without a formal credit check in some cases, or with affordability checks in others. The level of underwriting and protection can differ from a standard consumer loan.

Because BNPL is typically offered at the point of sale, it can feel different from applying for a loan in advance. That does not always mean it is simpler or cheaper. Borrowers should read the terms, including fees, interest and default handling.

Consumer protection and practical considerations

Regulators in Nigeria have been paying attention to digital lending and consumer complaints. The CBN supervises microfinance banks, while the FCCPC and CBN issued 2022 guidelines for digital money lenders. These are relevant to understanding the regulatory environment, though specific obligations may depend on the provider and product.

For consumers, it is important to understand the total cost of an instalment plan, the due dates and what happens if a repayment is missed. For merchants, it is important to understand the lender's terms, fees and reporting requirements.

Why instalment credit matters for Nigerian consumers and businesses

Instalment credit can make planned purchases more accessible. It can also help businesses sell higher-priced items. However, credit at checkout carries risk for both the consumer and the merchant. The consumer faces repayment obligations; the merchant faces the risk that the consumer may not repay.

In a country with a large informal sector and varying access to banking, credit needs may differ from those in more formal markets. Microfinance banks and digital lenders have both been part of the conversation around small-scale credit. The right structure for a particular buyer or business depends on the product, the terms and the circumstances.

For consumers, the main point is to compare options before committing. For businesses, the main point is to understand the cost of capital. BNPL can be a useful credit product, but it should be used within a clear understanding of terms and risk.

Key questions before choosing an instalment plan

Although this is general education, not personalised advice, it can be useful to consider key questions. What is the total cost of the purchase? How many instalments are required? When are payments due? What happens if a payment is missed? How does the provider handle disputes or complaints?

For merchants, similar questions arise about the lender's fees, approval process and reporting. The more transparent the terms, the easier it is for buyers and sellers to use instalment credit confidently.

Affordability and cash flow

Instalment credit can make a purchase affordable in the short term, but it does not remove the need for repayment capacity. A shopper may value a product but still need to ensure monthly repayments fit within their budget. A merchant may offer instalments to improve sales, but may still face defaults.

The final decision to use or offer BNPL should consider credit risk, cash flow, customer support and regulatory compliance.
