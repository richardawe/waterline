WhatsApp Reminders for Unpaid Bills: Effective Practices and Legal Limits in Nigeria

WhatsApp has become a popular channel for businesses to reach customers quickly, including for reminding them about unpaid bills. While the platform offers convenience, its use must align with Nigeria’s data‑protection and consumer‑protection frameworks to avoid legal risk and reputational harm.

Why WhatsApp is used for bill reminders

Many organisations choose WhatsApp because it delivers messages instantly, has high open rates, and allows two‑way communication without the cost of SMS or call centres. For recurring bills such as rent, school fees, or utility payments, a quick reminder can reduce delinquency and improve cash flow.

What works: best practices

Keep messages brief and factual

Effective reminders contain only the essential details: the amount owed, the due date, and how to pay. Avoid adding commentary, opinions, or unnecessary personal information.

Obtain consent or rely on existing contractual relationship

The NDPA 2023 requires a lawful basis for processing personal data. For most bill reminders, the existing contract for goods or services provides that basis, but it is good practice to confirm that the customer has agreed to receive WhatsApp notifications (e.g., via a sign‑up clause).

Limit frequency and timing

Sending a single reminder a few days before the due date, followed by a polite follow‑up after the deadline, is generally acceptable. Repeated messages throughout the day or late at night can be seen as harassment.

What crosses the line: prohibited practices

Sharing debtor info with third parties

Forwarding a debtor’s contact details to friends, family, or unrelated parties to pressure payment violates the NDPA’s data‑minimisation principle and may constitute unlawful disclosure.

Using WhatsApp to contact contacts or shame

The FCCPC has warned against practices that reach out to a borrower’s phone contacts or publish debt information to embarrass them. Such actions are considered unfair and can trigger enforcement action.

Sending misleading or threatening language

Messages that imply legal action will be taken immediately, falsely claim fees, or use intimidating tone exceed acceptable reminder content and may be deemed deceptive or abusive.

Legal framework: NDPA and FCCPC expectations

Lawful basis and data minimisation

Under the NDPA, organisations must identify a lawful basis (e.g., contract, consent) before processing personal data and must collect only what is necessary for the reminder purpose.

Security safeguards

Personal data shared via WhatsApp should be protected against unauthorised access. While WhatsApp provides end‑to‑end encryption, organisations should still ensure that devices are secured and that backups are handled safely.

FCCPC stance on harassment

The FCCPC treats any conduct that intimidates, embarrasses, or unreasonably pressures a consumer as unfair. This includes repeated unsolicited messages, contacting third parties, or using shame‑tactics.

Steps to stay compliant

Document consent and purpose

Maintain a record of how and when the customer agreed to receive WhatsApp notifications, and limit the use of that data to bill reminders only.

Use templates reviewed for tone

Create standard message templates that have been checked for neutrality, clarity, and compliance with both NDPA and FCCPC guidance.

Provide opt‑out mechanism

Include a simple way for recipients to stop receiving WhatsApp reminders (e.g., reply "STOP") and honour such requests promptly.

Keep records of communications

Log the date, time, and content of each reminder sent. This documentation helps demonstrate compliance if questioned by regulators.

By following these practices, Nigerian businesses can leverage WhatsApp’s reach for effective collections while respecting consumer rights and staying within the bounds of the law.

FAQ

Is it legal to send WhatsApp messages about unpaid bills in Nigeria?

Yes, sending reminders via WhatsApp is permissible if the organisation has a lawful basis for processing the debtor’s personal data, such as a contractual relationship, and follows the principles of the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023, including data minimisation and security.

What kind of WhatsApp content is considered acceptable for bill reminders?

Acceptable messages are brief, factual, and limited to the outstanding amount, due date, and payment instructions. They should avoid threatening language, shaming, or sharing the debtor’s information with unrelated parties.

Which practices could trigger action from the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC)?

The FCCPC has acted against lenders that harass borrowers by contacting their phone contacts, publishing debt details to shame them, or using misleading or threatening tone. Such conduct is deemed unfair to consumers under FCCPC guidelines.

How can a business ensure its WhatsApp reminder process complies with the NDPA?

Businesses should document the lawful basis for processing, limit data to what is necessary, secure the messages (e.g., using end‑to‑end encryption where possible), provide an easy opt‑out method, and retain records of consent and communications for accountability.
