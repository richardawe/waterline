How Loan Apps Use Your Phone Data, and What the Rules Allow in Nigeria

Many digital loan apps ask permission to read your contacts, text messages, call history, location and other phone data before you can complete an application. In Nigeria, that access is not unlimited: the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and rules from the FCCPC and CBN set boundaries on how lenders may collect, process and share personal information. Regulators have also acted against lenders that contact borrowers’ phone contacts to shame or pressure them. This article explains what data loan apps may request, what the law generally allows, and what borrowers can look out for.

What phone data loan apps typically request

When you install a loan app, it may ask for access to your contact list, SMS messages, call logs, GPS location, camera or gallery, and device identifiers. Lenders generally say they use this information to verify your identity, assess your ability to repay, and locate borrowers who fall behind on payments. Some of this data can help a lender build a picture of your financial behaviour where formal credit history is limited. However, an app requesting access to data does not automatically mean it needs all of that data to process your loan. Permission screens vary by operating system and by app, so the same lender might request different data on different devices.

What the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 allows

The Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023 governs how lenders and loan apps may collect, process and share borrowers’ personal data. It applies to any organisation that processes personal data in Nigeria, including digital lenders, and is enforced by the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC). Under the Act, processing personal data generally requires a lawful basis, and organisations are expected to follow principles such as data minimisation—collecting only what is necessary for a stated purpose—and putting security safeguards in place. The law also gives individuals certain rights over their data, such as the right to ask how information is being used, though the exact mechanics depend on the NDPC’s regulations and the organisation’s policies. These rules mean a lender should not collect more phone data than it reasonably needs for the loan, and should have a clear reason for sharing that data with any third party.

FCCPC and CBN oversight of digital lenders

The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC), together with the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), issued guidelines in 2022 requiring digital money lenders in Nigeria to register and be approved before operating. The move followed widespread complaints about predatory loan apps, and the guidelines remain in force. The CBN is Nigeria’s banking and monetary regulator, responsible for licensing deposit money banks and microfinance banks and setting the Monetary Policy Rate. While the CBN’s core licensing role covers banks and microfinance banks, the FCCPC’s consumer protection mandate extends to how digital lenders treat borrowers. Registration is intended to bring lenders under a common supervisory framework and make it easier for regulators to trace complaints back to an approved entity.

Contacting borrowers’ phone contacts

The FCCPC has acted against digital lenders that shamed or harassed borrowers, including contacting people in a borrower’s phone contacts. The Commission treats such practices as unfair to consumers. This means that accessing a contact list is one thing; using it to call or message a borrower’s friends, family or colleagues to pressure repayment may cross a line under consumer protection rules. Borrowers who experience this kind of contact are not without recourse, though the right step depends on the circumstances.

What borrowers can look out for

Before installing a loan app, it can help to check what permissions it requests and whether those permissions match the service it is offering. A privacy policy that explains what data is collected, why it is collected and who it is shared with is another useful indicator. Borrowers may also want to confirm whether a digital lender is registered or approved under the FCCPC and CBN framework, since operating without approval is contrary to the 2022 guidelines. Reading permission requests carefully can help a borrower spot when an app is asking for more than appears necessary for a simple loan application. None of these steps guarantees a particular outcome, and readers should consider their own situation before sharing sensitive phone data.

Who enforces the rules

The NDPC enforces the NDPA 2023 and handles matters related to unlawful data collection or insecure data handling. The FCCPC handles consumer protection concerns, including unfair or deceptive practices by digital lenders. If a borrower believes a lender has misused phone data or harassed contacts, these are the relevant regulatory bodies. This article does not provide legal advice, and anyone with a specific dispute may wish to speak with a qualified legal practitioner.

FAQ

Can a loan app access my phone contacts without permission?

Loan apps typically ask for permission to access contacts, messages and other phone data during installation. Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, processing personal data generally requires a lawful basis, and the FCCPC has treated contacting a borrower’s contacts to shame or pressure them as an unfair consumer practice.

Are digital lenders allowed to share my data with other companies?

The NDPA 2023 requires organisations that process personal data to have a lawful basis, limit collection to what is necessary, and apply security safeguards. Sharing data with third parties should be consistent with those rules and disclosed to the borrower.

Do all loan apps in Nigeria need to be registered?

The FCCPC and CBN issued guidelines in 2022 requiring digital money lenders in Nigeria to register and be approved before operating, and those guidelines remain in force.

Who enforces data protection rules for loan apps?

The Nigeria Data Protection Commission enforces the NDPA 2023, while the FCCPC handles consumer protection concerns involving digital lenders.

What can I do if a lender contacts my contacts to pressure repayment?
