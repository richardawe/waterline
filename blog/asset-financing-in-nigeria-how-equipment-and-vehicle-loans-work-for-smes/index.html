Asset Financing in Nigeria: How Equipment and Vehicle Loans Work for SMEs

Asset financing in Nigeria enables businesses to acquire essential equipment or vehicles without paying the full purchase price upfront. Instead, lenders provide funding that is repaid over an agreed period, typically with the financed asset serving as collateral. This guide explains how equipment and vehicle loans work, who provides them, and what SMEs should consider before borrowing.

What asset financing means for Nigerian SMEs

Asset financing refers to any lending arrangement where the loan is secured by a tangible asset. In the Nigerian SME context, this most commonly takes the form of equipment finance—covering machinery, manufacturing tools, ICT hardware, or agricultural implements—or vehicle finance for trucks, pickup cars, or commercial buses. While the two share similar structures, equipment loans often finance assets with longer useful lives, whereas vehicle finance is typically arranged faster around the asset's depreciation profile.

Typical loan structure: tenor, interest, and security

In a standard asset finance deal, the lender provides a significant portion of the asset's cost, and the borrower repays the balance plus interest over a fixed period. Tenors commonly range from one to several years, depending on the asset's useful life and the borrower's cash flow. Interest rates vary by lender, loan size, and the borrower's credit profile. The financed asset itself typically serves as primary collateral, meaning the lender can repossess it in case of default. Some lenders may also require additional security, especially for younger businesses or larger amounts.

Who provides asset finance in Nigeria?

A mix of deposit money banks, microfinance banks, and development finance institutions offer asset financing. Commercial banks often bundle equipment or vehicle finance into their SME or corporate banking desks, with approval processes that factor in business turnover, time in operation, and the asset's provenance. Microfinance banks cater to smaller ticket sizes and less-established businesses, sometimes requiring group guarantees or alternative credit data.

The Bank of Industry (BOI) is Nigeria's oldest and largest development finance institution, providing long-term, low-interest financing to SMEs and industrial projects, often channelled through participating commercial and microfinance banks. By supplying cheap long-term funds, the BOI helps reduce the cost of on-lending, making equipment and vehicle finance more accessible for businesses that might otherwise face higher rates from mainstream banks.

The application and approval process

Applying for asset finance typically begins with a business plan or project profile, a pro forma invoice or quotation for the intended asset, and recent financial statements. The lender will assess the business's repayment capacity, the asset's value and condition, and may conduct a site inspection or valuation. Upon approval, the loan offer outlines the repayment schedule and any covenants. Disbursement often goes directly to the asset supplier rather than the borrower's account, which reduces the risk of misapplication of funds.

Costs, risks, and questions to ask

Beyond the advertised interest rate, borrowers should ask about processing fees, early repayment penalties, and whether insurance (e.g., asset insurance or life cover) is mandatory. The main risk is repossession if repayments are missed, which can disrupt operations. SMEs should compare total cost of ownership, verify the lender's licensing, and read the fine print on covenants or cross-default clauses. It's also wise to check if the finance agreement allows for asset upgrades or early settlement without excessive cost.

Making the right choice for your business

Before committing, calculate the monthly outflow against your projected revenue, and consider whether hiring or leasing might be cheaper in the short term. Asset finance is best suited for businesses with steady cash flow that need to grow their productive capacity without depleting working capital. If the loan is for revenue-generating equipment, ensure the expected return comfortably covers the cost of borrowing.

Quick FAQ

Q: What is typical upfront payment for an equipment loan in Nigeria? A: Many lenders finance a large share of the asset cost, but a smaller upfront payment can improve approval odds and potentially affect the interest rate offered.

Q: How long does approval typically take for a vehicle loan? A: Timelines vary, but many lenders process vehicle finance applications within a few weeks, especially if quotations and bank statements are complete.

Q: Can a newly registered SME access asset financing? A: Yes, though options may be more limited. Microfinance banks and BOI-participating lenders often have products tailored for younger businesses, though they may require a guarantee or higher interest rate to compensate for perceived risk.

Q: Does the Bank of Industry lend directly to businesses? A: The BOI typically does not lend directly to every SME. Instead, it provides long-term, low-interest funds to participating commercial and microfinance banks, which on-lend to eligible businesses under BOI-guideline terms.
